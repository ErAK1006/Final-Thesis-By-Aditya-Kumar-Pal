## MULTIMODAL EMOTION RECOGNITION: INTEGRATING SPEECH, FACIAL EXPRESSIONS, AND BODY LANGUAGE IN A CULTURAL CONTEXT
### ABSTRACT
The fields of machine learning, data analytics, and artificial intelligence (AI) have brought 
transformative advancements across various domains, including healthcare. A critical challenge 
in healthcare is enhancing the accuracy and efficiency of disease diagnosis and patient care, 
which necessitates the integration of multi-modal data sources such as audio, video, and clinical 
records. This research investigates the application of multi-modal learning techniques to ana
lyse and predict emotional and behavioural states using audio and video data, with a specific 
emphasis on emotion recognition from multi-modal inputs. 
The study aims to develop a robust multi-modal system that integrates features from both audio 
and video modalities to predict human emotional states in healthcare-related applications. The 
research employs a deep learning framework, leveraging the MobileNetV2 model for video 
feature extraction and pre-trained models for audio data. The extracted features are fused to 
create a unified representation, facilitating emotion recognition tasks. 
Extensive experimentation demonstrates the effectiveness of multi-modal feature fusion in en
hancing the performance of emotion classification models. Using a dataset of emotion-labelled 
audio and video recordings, the study reveals significant improvements in performance over 
single-modal data. Results are evaluated using metrics such as accuracy, precision, recall, and 
F1-score. 
The findings highlight the potential of multi-modal learning techniques for applications in 
healthcare, where recognizing emotional and behavioural states is critical for diagnosis, ther
apy, and patient care. This research also underscores the importance of data preprocessing, fea
ture extraction, and model evaluation, offering valuable insights into the challenges and oppor
tunities in multi-modal emotion recognition systems. 
In conclusion, this study contributes to the growing body of knowledge in multi-modal learning 
and emotion recognition. It presents a scalable framework adaptable to diverse applications, 
including mental health monitoring, patient emotional well-being assessment, and human-com
puter interaction.
